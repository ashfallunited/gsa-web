Ashfall United is a football club and youth development organisation based in Monrovia, Liberia. We use sport for social impact—helping young people grow through football, leadership, education, and community support. On the pitch, we play with purpose: total football built on control, dominance, and discipline. Off the pitch, we exist to close the gap between talent and opportunity.

Rooted in Monrovia, led from the community

Liberia's future is young. In Monrovia—the capital and heart of our work—football is more than a game. It is language, identity, and hope. Ashfall United was built by people who know these communities: coaches, mentors, and leaders who believe that structured programmes, not luck, should decide who gets a chance.

In four years, we have reached more than 5,000 young people across the city. Our model is simple: talent is everywhere; access is not. We create access.

What makes our first team historic

Ashfall United's first team carries a distinction no other club in Liberia can claim: we are the first team in Liberia to field an all U-18 squad in the national third division league.

That is not a marketing line—it is a statement about who we are. We trust young players to compete at a senior level because we invest in them early: technically, tactically, and as leaders. Competing in the third division with an entirely under-18 roster shows what disciplined development can produce—and it gives the whole country a reason to watch what comes next from Monrovia.

Our style on the field reflects that belief. We teach total football: players comfortable in multiple roles, confident in possession, and committed to controlled, dominant football rather than chaos. We want to dictate tempo, earn respect through structure, and grow leaders who can organise those around them.

Three pillars of impact

Football brings young people through the door. What keeps them growing is the ecosystem around the pitch:

  • Sport & development — Structured coaching, leagues, tournaments, and pathways from community sites to academy and first-team football.
  • Health & wellbeing — Screenings, nutrition, mental health awareness, and injury prevention so players can stay on the pitch and in school.
  • Education & opportunity — Academic support, re-enrolment, life skills, and mentorship so sport connects to a future beyond the final whistle.

Each pillar reinforces the others. A player who learns leadership in training carries it into the classroom. A player supported in school stays in the programme long enough to benefit from our pathway.

Leadership is part of the curriculum

We do not assume leadership appears on its own. We teach it—on the pitch, in squad meetings, and through responsibility in community sessions. Captains are trained, not only named. Young players learn to communicate under pressure, support teammates, and represent the club with pride.

That approach is why an all U-18 team can compete in national league football: they are not only skilled; they are organised, accountable, and brave.

Who we work with

We collaborate with governments, international organisations, schools, and local institutions that share our belief in sport for development. Our partners and supporters make it possible to keep programmes free or affordable for the families who need them most.
